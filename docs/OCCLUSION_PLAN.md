## 0. Why this is not a cosmetic fix

The obvious framing — *"free-space FOV over-counts, so occlusion will lower `R_i`"* — is true but
misses the point. **Occlusion is a spatially varying correction whose magnitude is correlated with
built form, and built form is correlated with both camera placement and demographics.** It is
therefore a potential *confound*, not a nuisance rescaling, and **we cannot predict which way it
moves the disparity coefficient.**

Concretely. Most of the camera stock (Amnesty, ~29k of ~31k devices) is geocoded without a heading,
so `FrustumWedge::from_degrees(.., None, ..)` models it as a **360° disc of radius 15 m**
(`geometry.rs:40-46`). That disc has an area of ~707 m². In the Manhattan core, with near-100% lot
coverage, **most of that disc is inside buildings.** In detached-housing Staten Island, almost none
of it is. So the free-space model over-counts by maybe 2× in one place and 1.05× in another — and
those places differ systematically by race and income.

### The trap that makes 2D-only occlusion *worse than useless*

A naive 2D test blocks on any footprint, regardless of height. But a camera is mounted **~5 m up**,
and a pedestrian is **~1.7 m tall**. A 3 m garage between them does **not** block the sightline in
reality — and 2D says it does.

Low-rise built form (1–2 story houses, garages, sheds) is concentrated in the outer boroughs, which
are disproportionately **white and higher-income**. So a 2D-only model would **over-block exactly in
the low-`R_i`, high-white, high-income block groups** — suppressing their exposure more than it should
and thereby **artificially inflating the %Hispanic disparity we are trying to measure.**

> **That is a bias pointing toward our own headline finding. We must not ship it.**
> **This is why height-aware (2.5D) occlusion is not an optional refinement — it is the requirement.**

That argument is also the paper's justification for the whole exercise, and it belongs in the methods
section.

## 2. The three correctness traps

### 🚨 Trap 1 — a camera inside a footprint sees *nothing*

If a camera's apex is **inside** a building polygon, every ray from it to a street point crosses that
polygon's boundary exactly once ⇒ `segments_cross` ⇒ `sightline_blocked` ⇒ **the camera is blind.**
It silently contributes zero. `R_i` drops, and it *looks like occlusion working*.

This is the failure mode most likely to ship undetected. It must be measured before anything else.

How many cameras are affected? Unknown — **that measurement is Phase 0**. A priori most apexes should
be in the roadbed (Amnesty = intersection centroids; Dahir = GSV panorama sample points, i.e. where the
survey car was; DOT/DeFlock/enforcement = poles and signs). But geocoding error and building overhangs
will put some inside.

**Physical model:** a camera mounted on a building **looks out from its facade** — its own host
building must never occlude it.
**Rule:** at index-build time, point-in-polygon each camera apex; if inside polygon `P`, record
`host: Option<u32>` and **exclude `P`'s edges from that camera's occluder set**.
**Requires:** edges carry a polygon id (`EdgeSeg { a, b, poly: u32 }`).
**Must be disclosed in the paper:** *"N of ~31,000 camera groups geocode inside a building footprint;
we treat them as facade-mounted and exclude the host building."*
**Bound it:** ship a sensitivity variant that instead **drops** inside-building cameras entirely. If
the headline is stable across the two, the choice does not matter.

### 🚨 Trap 2 — 2D over-blocks in low-rise areas (see §0)

The `BuildingFootprints` asset has no height, so a naive wiring is 2D-only and carries a demographic
bias pointing **toward our own finding**. Height is mandatory. See Phase 1.

### ⚠️ Trap 3 — targets inside buildings

Walkshed sample points are CSCL street-centerline vertices, so they are normally in the roadbed. But
CSCL includes tunnels and streets running *under* buildings (Park Ave through the MetLife building; the
Queens-Midtown Tunnel). A target inside a footprint will be occluded — which is arguably **correct**
(a street camera cannot see you inside a tunnel). Leave the behaviour, but **count** these points and
report the number. If it is large, something is wrong with the graph, not the occluder.

## 3. Architecture

### 3.1 Replace the flat slice with an index

`sightline_blocked` is **O(|occluders|)**. Citywide there are ~1.08M footprints ⇒ roughly **8–12M
edges** after RDP. Passing that as a slice is a non-starter: the inner loop in `walkshed_exposure`
runs (walkshed points × sensors) times.

**Decision: a uniform grid, not an R-tree.**

Rationale — and this is a real trade-off, not a coin flip:
- `sim-core` compiles to **WASM**. A grid adds no dependency; `rstar` would be a new one there.
- Queries are **short segments** (≤ 30 m, bounded by the max camera range). An R-tree bbox query on a
  diagonal 30 m segment returns everything in a 30×30 m box — in dense Manhattan that is a lot of
  false positives. A **DDA walk along the segment** touches only the 2–4 cells the ray actually
  crosses. Tighter, and cache-friendly.
- Build is O(n), no tree construction, fully deterministic.
- There is precedent: `group_sensors` (`scenario.rs:81`) already grid-buckets for union-find.

```rust
// sim-core/src/occlusion.rs  (new)

/// One wall segment, tagged with the polygon it belongs to (so a camera can
/// exclude its own host building — see Trap 1).
#[derive(Clone, Copy)]
struct EdgeSeg { a: Vec2, b: Vec2, poly: u32, height_m: f32 }

/// Uniform-grid spatial index over building-footprint wall segments.
/// An EMPTY index short-circuits every query to `false`, so the no-occlusion
/// path stays free and the existing behaviour is exactly recoverable.
pub struct OccluderIndex {
    cell_m: f64,
    origin: Vec2,
    dims: (i32, i32),
    cells: Vec<Vec<u32>>,   // cell -> edge indices
    edges: Vec<EdgeSeg>,
}

pub struct Sightline {
    pub from: Vec2,
    pub to: Vec2,
    pub h_from_m: f64,      // camera mount height
    pub h_to_m: f64,        // target (pedestrian) height
    pub exclude_poly: Option<u32>,   // the camera's host building, if any
}

impl OccluderIndex {
    pub fn empty() -> Self;
    pub fn build(footprints: &[BuildingFootprints], cell_m: f64) -> Self;

    /// True if any wall blocks the sightline. Walks only the grid cells the
    /// segment actually crosses (DDA), and skips `exclude_poly`.
    pub fn blocked(&self, s: &Sightline) -> bool;

    /// O(n) reference implementation over every edge — slow, obviously correct.
    /// Used ONLY by the equivalence property test (see §5, V2).
    pub fn blocked_reference(&self, s: &Sightline) -> bool;

    /// Point-in-polygon over the indexed footprints; the host building of a
    /// camera apex, if it sits inside one. (Trap 1.)
    pub fn containing_polygon(&self, p: Vec2) -> Option<u32>;
}
```

**API change:** `&[OccluderEdge]` → `&OccluderIndex` across `captures`, `walkshed_exposure`,
`direct_capture_exposure`, and the ~10 batch/app call sites. Keep
`sightline_blocked(from, to, &[OccluderEdge])` untouched so the existing `geometry.rs` unit tests
still compile and pass.

### 3.2 The 2.5D height test

Replace the boolean `segments_cross` with a version that returns the crossing parameter:

```rust
/// Parameter t ∈ (0,1) along (a1→a2) where it crosses (b1,b2); None if no proper crossing.
pub fn segment_cross_t(a1: Vec2, a2: Vec2, b1: Vec2, b2: Vec2) -> Option<f64>;
```

Then a wall blocks iff it is **taller than the sightline where the sightline crosses it**:

```
t          = segment_cross_t(from, to, wall.a, wall.b)?
line_h(t)  = h_from + t * (h_to - h_from)      // linear interpolation
blocked    = wall.height_m > line_h(t)
```

Defaults (all parameterizable, all swept in §6):
- `h_cam = 5.0 m` — typical NYC pole/facade mount. Sweep **{3, 5, 8}**.
- `h_tgt = 1.7 m` — a standing adult. Sweep **{1.0, 1.7}** (torso vs. head: how much of a person must
  be visible to count as captured?).

`segment_cross_t` must preserve the existing semantics at the boundary: **collinear / touching ⇒ not
crossing**, so a camera geocoded exactly on a facade does not self-block.

## 5. Validation

**V1 — unit tests** (extend `geometry.rs` / new `occlusion.rs`):
apex inside a polygon; apex exactly on a facade (must **not** self-block); ray grazing a corner
(clear); target inside a polygon; wall shorter than the sightline (**not** blocked); wall taller
(blocked); wall exactly at sightline height; degenerate zero-length walls; a ray that exits and
re-enters the same concave polygon.

**V2 — ⭐ index-vs-reference equivalence (the most important test).**
Property test: for ≥100k random `(from, to)` pairs drawn over the real NYC footprint set,
assert `index.blocked(s) == index.blocked_reference(s)`. This validates the fast grid path against the
O(n) obviously-correct path and will catch every DDA / cell-assignment / boundary bug. **If this test
does not exist, the occlusion numbers cannot be trusted.**

**V3 — geometric sanity.** Fraction of each camera's 15 m disc that is interior to buildings, by
borough. Must be high in Manhattan, low in Staten Island. A monotone ordering is the smell test.

**V4 — visual validation.** Render visibility polygons for ~50 cameras over the basemap; eyeball
against Street View. Slow, but it is the only test that checks the model against *reality* rather than
against itself. Produces the paper figure.

**V5 — Amnesty mount type.** Amnesty's methodology records mount type (streetlight / signal / pole vs.
**building** vs. other). If that field survives into the baked asset (⚠️ **needs checking** — it may
have been dropped, in which case a re-bake could recover it), then **building-mounted cameras should
have a much higher rate of testing "inside a footprint"** than pole-mounted ones. That is an
independent empirical check on our Trap-1 detection, using data we already have.

**V6 — ⭐ the differential-bias analysis (the scientific payoff).**
Per block group, compute `occlusion_ratio_i = R_i^occl / R_i^free`. Then:
- Map it, and report its distribution by borough.
- Regress it on demographics **and** built form (lot coverage — computable from the footprints
  themselves — and mean building height):
  `occlusion_ratio_i ~ %Hispanic + %Black + income + lot_coverage + mean_height`
- **If `occlusion_ratio` has a demographic gradient, the free-space model was differentially biased,
  and the disparity estimate moves.** Report the coefficient before and after, and decompose how much
  of the change is built form vs. residual.
- This is a **finding**, and it is the honest answer to "why did your numbers change?"

## 8. Coupling with the other P0 — recall

Occlusion and the (currently inert) recall correction **push in opposite directions**: occlusion
lowers `R_i`; recall correction (≈50%, so a ~2× inflation) raises it. They are independent, and
fixing only one produces a number that is wrong in a *known* direction.

**Recommendation: fix both, and report the 2×2** (free/occluded × raw/recall-corrected). Then the
paper can state exactly what `R_i` is, and the sensitivity to each choice is legible. Doing occlusion
alone would leave the methods section still saying something untrue.

## 10. 🔴 The coupled over-count we found while scoping this

**The Amnesty source carries mount type, camera type, and decoder agreement — and the bake throws all
of it away.** `data/snapshots/amnesty/counts_per_intersections.csv`:

```
PanoramaId, n_cameras_median,
attached_street_median, attached_building_median, attached_unknown_median,
type_dome_median,       type_bullet_median,       type_unknown_median,
n_cameras_agreement, ...(per-field agreement)..., Lat, Long, BoroName, URL, ImageDate
```

`data-pipeline/src/amnesty.rs` reads only `n_cameras_median` and the coordinates. Two consequences,
and the second is **larger than occlusion**:

### 10a. `attached_building` / `attached_street` — solves Trap 1 with *data*

A camera with `attached_building_median > 0` **is** facade-mounted. Its host building must be excluded
from its occluder set **by construction**, not by a geocoding-error heuristic. A camera with
`attached_street_median > 0` is on a pole in the roadbed — if it geocodes *inside* a footprint, that
is a genuine error to repair, not a facade camera to accommodate. This turns Trap 1 from guesswork
into a data-driven rule, and it makes **V5 a real validation** rather than an aspiration.

### 10b. `type_dome` vs `type_bullet` — **measured 2026-07-13, and the premise below was wrong**

> ## ✅ ANSWERED: there are **no bullet directions** in the Amnesty data, and 84% of typed cameras are domes
>
> Two questions had to be settled before any re-bake. Both now are, straight from
> `data/snapshots/amnesty/counts_per_intersections.csv` (43,406 panoramas, 28,954 cameras):
>
> **1. Is a heading recorded anywhere?** **No.** The schema is 21 columns and *not one* carries a
> bearing, heading, direction, azimuth or orientation. The only fields are per-panorama *counts*:
> `n_cameras`, `attached_{street,building,unknown}`, `type_{dome,bullet,unknown}`, each with an
> annotator-`_agreement` flag, plus lat/lon/pano-id/borough/URL/date. So even for a camera we *know*
> is a bullet, **we do not know which way it points, and no re-bake can recover it.** The
> facade-normal prior below is the only route to a heading — and it is an *inference*, not data.
>
> **2. How many are bullets anyway?** **Far fewer than assumed, and mostly unlabeled:**
>
> | | cameras | share of all | share of *typed* |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | dome (omnidirectional) | 2,266 | 7.8% | **84%** |
> | **bullet (directional)** | **444** | **1.5%** | **16%** |
> | unknown | 63 | 0.2% | — |
> | **no type label at all** | **24,458** | **84.5%** | — |
>
> Only **4,496 of 28,954 cameras (15.5%)** sit on a panorama where the type sub-question was answered
> at all. Among those that were, **domes outnumber bullets 5:1** — so the current 360°-disc model is
> *correct* for the large majority and wrong for a minority, rather than wrong for "most" as claimed.
> (Mount type, by contrast, *is* well populated: `attached_building` 22,133 / 76.4%,
> `attached_street` 3,317 / 11.5% — so the facade-normal idea still has data to stand on.)
>
> **Verdict: this is no longer "plausibly bigger than occlusion."** Expected over-count is on the order
> of 16% of cameras × (1 − θ/360) ≈ **12% of the CCTV stock's coverage**, not 4–5× — and it is
> *unfixable by direction* because the direction does not exist. It should be modeled as an
> **expectation over unknown heading**, not "fixed" by a re-bake.

~~`FrustumWedge::from_degrees(.., heading: None, ..)` ⇒ a 360° disc, and the data distinguishes dome
from bullet, so we over-count bullets 4–5×.~~ The first clause is true; the inference was not.

### The principled model — and it lands on the same structure *again*

A directional camera with **unknown, uniformly-random** heading `Θ` sees a set of street points `S`
iff `Θ` falls inside the union of FOV-windows centred on the bearings to each point of `S`:

```
P(camera sees you anywhere on the walk) = measure( ⋃_{p ∈ S} [bearing(p) − θ/2, bearing(p) + θ/2] ) / 360
```

— the Minkowski dilation of the bearing-set by `θ/2`, exactly computable by sorting bearings and
merging arcs on the circle. Note what it implies:

- **At a single point** (a *place*-based measure), `P = θ/360 ≈ 0.19` for a 70° bullet. A four-to-five-fold
  over-count — exactly the fear above.
- **Over a walkshed** (a *path*-based measure), the street points near a camera subtend a wide arc, so
  the union of windows covers most of the circle and **`P → 1`**. The over-count largely evaporates.

**This is the third independent instance of the paper's thesis.** Occlusion, camera-type uncertainty,
and the headline activity-space result all say the same thing: *a quantity measured over a trajectory
is structurally robust to geometry that wrecks the same quantity measured at a point.* It arrived here
unbidden, from the data refusing to give us a heading — which makes it worth reporting, not hiding.

**Revised recommendation:** implement (1) the arc-union expectation as the primary model, since it is
honest about what we do not know and requires no new data; and (2) the **facade-normal prior** as a
sensitivity variant for the 76% of cameras flagged `attached_building` — the footprint geometry is
already loaded for occlusion, so the outward normal is free. Report both. Do **not** describe this as a
correction that "fixes" a bug; it is a re-parameterization under acknowledged uncertainty.

## 14. 🔴 Found while unblocking the drive graph: **`R_i` was flooded over a car network**

Not an occlusion issue, but discovered by the same thread and it moves the headline, so it is recorded
here until it gets its own doc.

**`graph_nyc.osgraph` is the citywide *drive* graph.** `tools/bake_citywide.sh` bakes it with the full
drivability blacklist (`--cscl … - $PARKS $OPENST`). There was never a "missing" NYC drive graph — but
there was no citywide **pedestrian** graph at all, and every walkshed in the paper was being flooded
over the car network. (Manhattan-only builds were fine: `graph_manhattan.osgraph` is a true OSM walk
network. This is the five-borough scaling gap, again.)

What that means concretely, measured with `batch graph-stats`:

| | drive graph | walk graph |
|---|---|---|
| Highway | **372 km** | — |
| Ramp | **351 km** | — |
| Path/Trail | — | **540 km** |
| Boardwalk | — | 17 km |
| Step | — | 7.6 km |
| nodes / edges | 74,692 / 111,827 | 78,947 / 115,784 |

So a "10-minute walk" could flood **724 km of limited-access highway and ramp** — counting cameras that
overlook the Cross Bronx as cameras that saw you on your walk — while being unable to reach **564 km**
of park path, boardwalk and step that pedestrians actually use.

### The fix — `CsclNetwork::{Drive, Walk}`

CSCL carries a clean flag for each direction, and they are duals:

| network | keeps | drops via |
|---|---|---|
| Drive | roadway | `trafdir == NV` ("not for cars") |
| Walk | footway | `nonped == V` ("not for pedestrians") |

The **Brooklyn Bridge** proves it: its roadway is `(trafdir=FT, nonped=V)` and its promenade is
`(trafdir=NV, nonped=D)`, so each network keeps exactly one. The **Verrazzano**, which has no pedestrian
path, is `nonped=V` on 120 of 121 segments and simply does not exist on foot — so the walk graph leaves
Staten Island as its own component rather than stitching a phantom footway across the Narrows. The walk
graph also *keeps* park interiors and Open Streets, which the drive graph masks out — those are car
-exclusion layers, and a car-free street is precisely where pedestrians are.
