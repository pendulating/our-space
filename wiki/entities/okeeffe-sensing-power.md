# O'Keeffe et al., PNAS 2019

**O'Keeffe, Anjomshoaa, Strogatz, Santi & Ratti. "Quantifying the sensing power of
vehicle fleets." *PNAS* 116(26): 12752–12757, 2019.**
doi:10.1073/pnas.1821667116

In Manhattan, ~10 random taxis cover a third of street segments in a day.

This is the paper's most important — and most dangerous — ancestor. They compute
**coverage** (supply-side: what fraction of the network the fleet sees) and frame
it as a public good (air quality, road quality). We invert both moves:
**exposure** (what fraction of a person's day the fleet sees) and distributional
incidence of surveillance power.

Intro one-liner: *O'Keeffe et al. asked how much of the city the fleet can see.
We ask how much of you it sees — and whether it sees some people more than
others.*
