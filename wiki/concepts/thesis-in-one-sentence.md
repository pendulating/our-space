# Thesis in One Sentence

Surveillance exposure is a property of **trajectories**, not **places**. Measured
that way, two facts hold at once in New York City:

1. Camera *placement* bias is racialized, robust, and larger than place-based
   measures suggest ([[results-placement-bias]]).
2. *Lived* exposure has converged across race and income, because the commons is
   saturated ([[results-equalization]]).

Equal exposure is not equity. It is surveillance creep. A residence-based measure
cannot see it. This tension between placement intent and lived outcome is the
paper's most interesting sentence ([[measurement-paper-thesis]]).

The framing inverts Jane Jacobs: her "eyes on the street" were reciprocal, local,
interventionist, and accountable. Digital eyes invert every term, and mobile eyes
add a fifth failure — the eye no longer stays. See [[jacobs-inversion]].
