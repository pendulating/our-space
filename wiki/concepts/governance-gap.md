# The Governance Gap (C5)

Place-based governance cannot reach a moving camera.

## What exists
- **POST Act** — reaches NYPD only.
- **Signage-based notice** — a sign on a building cannot notify someone passing in
  a vehicle's field of view.
- **MTA ACE statute** (NY VTL §1111-c / §1111-c-1) — the sharpest normative
  contrast available. As of July 2026: 67 routes, >1,900 ACE-equipped buses,
  ~810 route-miles (⚠️ verify by hand; mta.info blocks fetching; cite with access
  date). The statute mandates ≥50 mobile bus-lane photo devices, **bans biometric /
  facial recognition outright**, caps retention at 1 year, limits purpose to
  adjudication, and bars the MTA itself from accessing images.

## The contrast
ACE is the only fleet with a statutory purpose limitation, retention cap, and FRT
ban. Nexar dashcams, Tesla Sentry, and private ALPR have none. And from the
sidewalk you cannot tell them apart. Pair with Brayne's directed-vs-dragnet frame:
ACE is statutorily *directed*; the commercial fleets are latent *dragnet*.

The paper proposes **trajectory-based transparency**: notice and accountability
attached to the camera's path through space, not its mounting point.
